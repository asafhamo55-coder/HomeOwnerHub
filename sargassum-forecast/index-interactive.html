Typical seasonal cycle (2011–2024 average)

Annual peak bloom — Great Atlantic Sargassum Belt (million tonnes)

Regional phasing — the belt drifts west & north (self-normalised, each region to its own peak)

The open Atlantic nursery leads (peak Jun–Jul), the Caribbean follows (~Jul–Aug), and the Gulf of Mexico lags last (~Aug–Sep) as Sargassum rides the Yucatán/Loop Current. The eastern Atlantic off West Africa runs counter-phase — a winter aggregation zone (peak Feb–Mar) that re-seeds the next summer bloom. At peak, the open Atlantic holds ~80% of belt biomass, the Caribbean ~15%, and the Gulf only ~4% — so the Gulf's absolute load is small even when its risk is high locally. Curves are modeled from USF/NOAA bulletins & Wang et al. 2019.

Methodology & sources. Risk is derived from (1) the monthly sargassum abundance climatology averaged over 2011–2024, (2) each site's documented baseline exposure to the Great Atlantic Sargassum Belt, and (3) the prevailing surface-current / wind drift that carries pelagic Sargassum through the Caribbean into the Gulf of Mexico. Bloom magnitudes referenced from the USF Optical Oceanography Lab Sargassum Watch System (SaWS) and the monthly Outlook of Sargassum blooms bulletins; satellite detection uses MODIS/VIIRS. Colour-coded presentation inspired by sargassummonitoring.com. Figures for 2025–2026 are model projections, not measurements — verify against the latest USF bulletin before operational use.
