Typical seasonal cycle (2011–2024 average)

Annual peak bloom — Great Atlantic Sargassum Belt (million tonnes)

Regional phasing — the belt drifts west & north (self-normalised, each region to its own peak)

The open Atlantic nursery leads (peak Jun–Jul), the Caribbean follows (~Jul–Aug), and the Gulf of Mexico lags last (~Aug–Sep) as Sargassum rides the Yucatán/Loop Current. The eastern Atlantic off West Africa runs counter-phase — a winter aggregation zone (peak Feb–Mar) that re-seeds the next summer bloom. At peak, the open Atlantic holds ~80% of belt biomass, the Caribbean ~15%, and the Gulf only ~4%. Modeled from USF/NOAA bulletins & Wang et al. 2019.

Methodology & sources. Risk = monthly 2011–2024 sargassum climatology × each site's baseline exposure to the Great Atlantic Sargassum Belt, capped at the site's known seasonal-peak tier, reflecting the surface-current / wind drift that carries pelagic Sargassum through the Caribbean into the Gulf. Bloom magnitudes from the USF Optical Oceanography Lab Sargassum Watch System (SaWS) monthly bulletins (satellite detection: MODIS/VIIRS); belt geometry from Wang et al. (2019), Science 365:83–87.
