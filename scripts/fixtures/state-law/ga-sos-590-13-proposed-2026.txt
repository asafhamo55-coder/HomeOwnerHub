Rule 590-13-1-.01. Definitions
All definitions contained in Section 43-17A-1 of the Official Code of Georgia Annotated
("Code" or "O.C.G.A.") are incorporated by reference herein. When used in the Act and in these
Rules, the below terms shall have the following meanings:
(1) "Act" means the Georgia Property Owners' Bill of Rights Act as set forth in Chapter 17A
of Title 43 of the Official Code of Georgia Annotated. (O.C.G.A. §§ 43-17A-1et seq.).
(2) "Affected Person" means a Resident or Owner who resides in an Owners' Development
whose Owners' Association is subject to a hearing under O.C.G.A. § 43-17A-4.
(3) "Agent" means any person authorized to act on behalf of an Owner, Resident, or Owners'
Association, including but not limited to, a management company, advocate, attorney, or
collection agency.
(4) "Applicant" means an Owners' Association submitting an application for a registration
pursuant to O.C.G.A. § 43-17A-2.
(5) "Assets" means a resource or property, tangible or intangible, that has economic or
physical value to which a person or entity has legal or equitable rights.
(6) "Board" means the governing body of an Owners' Association as designated in its
governing documents.
(7) "Business Day(s)" means any day other than Saturday, Sunday and State of Georgia
holidays.
(8) "Complainant" means an Owner or Resident who files a complaint as per O.C.G.A. § 43-
17A-5.
(9) "Day" means calendar day.
(10) "Digital Asset" means any electronic account, system, domain, page, profile, database,
content, data set, telephone number or credential created, acquired, maintained, branded,
promoted or used primarily for association business.
(11) "Director" means the Director of the Securities and Business Regulation Division of the
Office of the Secretary of State.
(12) "Discovery" means a formal process after a request for hearing is filed but prior to a
hearing where parties in a conflict file motions, issue subpoenas, or utilize other legal
mechanisms to gather information from other individuals.
(13) "Division" means the Division of Securities and Business Regulation of the Office of the
Secretary of State.
(14) "Filing" means the submission of a document to the Office of the Secretary of State by an
external party for purposes of effectuating an action under the Act.
(15) "Filing Fee" means an amount owed to the Office of the Secretary of State in connection
with the filing of a registration, renewal, amendment, or other filing required under the Act.
This does not include Administrative Services Fees as described in O.C.G.A. § 43-17A-4(i)
and § 43-17A-5(d).
(16) "The Office of the Secretary of State" means the Office of the Secretary of State of the
State of Georgia and all Divisions encompassed thereunder.
(17) "Personally Identifiable Information" or "PII" means information that can be used to
identify an individual, either directly or indirectly. This includes data that, when combined
with other available information, could reasonably allow someone to trace or distinguish a
person's identity.
(18) "Referee" means an individual appointed by the Secretary of State pursuant to O.C.G.A.
43-17A-4(f)(1) to preside over and prepare a report relating to hearings held under 43-17A-
4.
(19) "Registered Address" means the address of the Owners' Association as listed on an active
registration filed pursuant to the Act.
(20) "Registration Statement" or "Registration" means a functional document which, when
active, permits Owners' Associations to complete all acts permitted under the Act.
(21) "Residing in" means lawfully occupying a dwelling as a Resident or being an Owner of a
dwelling in an Owners' Development.
(22) "Resident" means a natural person who lawfully occupies a dwelling in an Owners'
Development.
(23) "Respondent" means an individual or Owners' Association requesting a hearing under
O.C.G.A. §43-17A-4 to challenge an order issued by the Office of Secretary of State.
(24) "Rules" means these rules and any others promulgated pursuant to the Act.
(25) "The Secretary of State" means the Secretary of State of the State of Georgia.

b. Records or documents that are not final; and
c. Secret-ballot selections.

Owners' Associations may redact exempt information
segregable, nonexempt portions,
including, but not limited to:
a. Account numbers;
b. Personal contact information or other PII; and
c. Communications that are privileged under applicable law.

Rule 590-13-2-.03. Request for Records or Documents maintained by Owners' Associations
(1) Requests for records or documents maintained pursuant to this Act may be submitted for any
reason or purpose, unless such request is otherwise prohibited by an order of a court of
competent jurisdiction. However:
a. An Owner may not request records or documents relating to specific assessments or
collection actions relating to persons who do not reside in the Owner's residence.
b. An Owners' Association is not required to respond to requests from persons who do not
reside in the Owners' Development.
(2) Requests for Owners' Association's records or documents must be in writing and sufficiently
detailed to allow the Owners' Association to determine the specific records or documents being
requested.
a. Requests for records or documents shall be submitted to the contact information provided
in the registration on file with the Office of the Secretary of State.
(3) Requests for records or documents maintained by an Owners' Association pursuant to the Act
shall be acknowledged in writing within five (5) business days and made available to a
requesting Owner within ten (10) days of acknowledgement of the Owner's written request.
a. If unusual circumstances prevent complete production within ten (10) days of
acknowledgement, the Owners' Association shall provide written notice stating the reason
for an extension, the records or documents affected, records or documents that can be
produced sooner, and a date certain for completion.
b. Delay by an Owners' Association's Agent is not good cause for an extension absent
providential cause or excusable neglect.
(4) Permissible Charges for records or documents.
a. Charges for an Owners' Association's records or documents shall be limited to actual,
reasonable, and documented costs.
b. Electronic transmission shall be provided without charge when no material staff time or
media cost is required.
C.
The Owners' Association shall give an advance itemized estimate before charges exceed
$25.00 and shall not condition access to undisputed records or documents on payment of a
disputed charge.
(5) This rule does not apply to requests or subpoenas issued by the Office of the Secretary of State
or their designees.

Rule 590-13-3-.01: Initial Application and Deficient Registrations
(1) Upon initial submission of an application for registration, an Owners' Association will be
issued a temporary registration effective immediately.
a. Temporary registrations will be effective for thirty (30) days and may be extended at the
discretion of the Director.
b. If the Division verifies that the application is not deficient and all filing fees have been
paid, the registration will be moved from a temporary registration to an annual registration.
c. Once advised of any deficiencies or failure to pay the filing fee, the Owners' Association
must remedy within thirty (30) days.
(2) Any registration application filed pursuant to the Act may be deemed deficient if any of the
following conditions exist:
a. the application is not completed on the proper form prescribed by the Secretary of State
electronically or in writing;
b. the application is incomplete;
c. required documentation is irrelevant or otherwise incomplete;
d. the associated filing fee is not properly submitted and paid; or
e. the application is not in compliance with any provision of the Act or associated rules.

(1) An application for renewal of registration may be filed at any time within ninety (90) days

Rule 590-13-4-.01. Enforcement and Investigatory Procedures
(1) The Secretary of State or designee may investigate the actions of any Owners' Association or
Agent acting on behalf of an Owners' Association to determine whether any person has
violated or is about to violate the Act or any rule, regulation, or order created under the Act or
to aid in the violation of the Act.
(2) All examinations and audits of the financial records or documents pursuant to the Act
conducted by the Secretary of State or their designee shall not be subject to public inspection,
unless the Secretary of State or designee determines it to be in the public interest to release
examinations and audits, or unless otherwise required by law.
(3) The Secretary of State or designee may refer any alleged violations of the Act or other laws to
any federal or state regulator, prosecutor, or other appropriate law enforcement agency.
(4) The Director may appoint a qualified auditor, examiner, or regulatory monitor to assess any
records or documents held by an Owners' Association or their Agent or otherwise obtained by
the Office of the Secretary of State.

Rule 590-13-5-.02: Appointment of Referee
(1) The Secretary of State or Director may at his or her discretion appoint a Referee to preside
over hearings held pursuant to O.C.G.A § 43-17A-4.
(2) Any Referee appointed shall possess the necessary skills and expertise to properly analyze the
evidence and prepare a comprehensive report.
(3) The Secretary of State or the Director shall require the submission of a résumé, completion of
a criminal background check, and any other information deemed necessary to evaluate the
Referee's qualifications.
(4) The Referee must certify that they do not have a conflict of interest in the matter. If a Referee
identifies a conflict between themselves and either party, they must advise the Secretary of
State's Office within two (2) business days of appointment in writing. The Secretary of State's
Office shall timely determine if disqualification and appointment of a new Referee is
appropriate.
(5) The Secretary of State or Director shall appoint by order which shall:
a. State the matter;
b. State the qualifications of the individual appointed as Referee, including sufficient
information to allow the parties to determine if there may be a conflict of interest;
c. Define reporting requirements; and
d. Establish the rate of compensation and invoicing procedures.

Rule 590-13-5-.03: Pre-Hearing Processes
(1) If a hearing is requested by a Respondent pursuant to O.C.G.A. § 43-17A-4, the Secretary of
State or designee shall select a Presider to oversee the hearing. The Presider shall issue a
Notice of Hearing to the Director and the Owners' Association associated with the order that
is being challenged.
a. Notices of Hearing will be publicly posted by the Secretary of State's Office.
(2) The Secretary of State or designee shall select a Clerk to handle administrative portions of the
hearing.
a. The Clerk shall maintain and furnish to the parties on the Notice of the Hearing the name,
service address, and telephone number of each party.
(3) Service of Process.
a. All filings submitted to the Presider shall be filed with the Clerk and the parties included
on the Notice of Hearing.
b. All filings shall be signed by the party or Agent making the filing and shall state the name,
address, telephone number and the representative capacity of the person making the
submission.
c. Service shall be made by first-class mail or electronic delivery to the addressees on the
Notice of Hearing.
(4) Communications with the Presider by Parties.
a. No party shall communicate with the Presider regarding the proceeding without including
the other party until it has been resolved, except for administrative, procedural purposes,
or emergencies that do not deal with substantive matters of the case.
b. This rule does not include normal and customary contact between the Secretary of State
and their staff with a Presider not relating to the contested case.

Rule 590-13-5-.04: Time
(1) Time limits begin on the start of the next business day after the event that triggered it.
(2) Three (3) days shall be added to any deadline period if the filing is served by mail.
(3) The Presider may adjust any time limit prescribed by these rules that are not otherwise
specified by law. The Presider shall notify all parties of any determination to change any time
limit in writing.
(4) A continuance may be requested by either party and may be approved at the Presider's sole
discretion.

Rule 590-13-5-.08: Hearing Procedures
(1) In any hearing conducted under O.C.G.A. § 43-17A-4, the Presider shall make an independent
determination on the basis of the evidence presented.
(2) The standard of proof on all issues shall be a preponderance of the evidence. The Respondent shall bear
the burden of proof in all matters relating to an order of the Secretary of State.
(3) Representation.
a. Any Respondent or affected person may appear at a hearing conducted under O.C.G.A. §
43-17A-4 "pro se", on their own behalf, without representation by an attorney.
b. Any Respondent or affected person may appear at a hearing conducted under O.C.G.A. §
43-17A-4 represented by an attorney duly licensed or otherwise permitted to practice in
Georgia.
c. Any Respondent may have an Agent act on its behalf to file, appear, or otherwise assist at
a hearing conducted under O.C.G.A. § 43-17A-4.
i. Prior to taking any action under this rule, an Agent must submit a fully executed form
prescribed by the Secretary of State indicating they are permitted to act on the behalf
of the Respondent.
1. Such form must be signed by a Board Member or the individual challenging the
order
d. An affected person may have an Agent act on its behalf to file, appear, or otherwise assist
at a hearing conducted under O.C.G.A. § 43-17A-4.
i. Prior to taking any action under this rule, an Agent must submit a fully executed form
prescribed by the Secretary of State indicating they are permitted to act on the behalf
of the affected person.
(4) The Presider shall conduct a fair and impartial hearing, take action to avoid unnecessary delay
in the disposition of the proceedings, and maintain order.
(5) Record of Hearings:
a. Any hearing conducted under O.C.G.A. § 43-17A-4 may be conducted virtually if the
record reflects that the Respondent and the Secretary of State or designee have consented
in writing.
b. Hearings shall be stenographically reported verbatim or recorded.
(6) If a party fails to attend a hearing held pursuant O.C.G.A. § 43-17A-4 after having been given
written notice, the Presider may proceed with the hearing in the absence of the party.
(7) Whenever a Presider determines that an expedited time frame is necessary to protect the

Rule 590-13-6-.01. Applicability and Scope of Rules
(1) The rules in this chapter govern hearings held under O.C.G.A. § 43-17A-5. If a hearing is held,
the hearing will be conducted before the Secretary of State, the Director, or another individual
selected by the Secretary of State. The term "Hearing Officer" shall refer to the Secretary of
State or the Director, or an appointed individual.
(2) Computation of Time.
a. Time limits begin on the start of the next business day after the event that triggered it.
b. Three (3) days shall be added to any deadline period if the filing is served by mail.
c. The Hearing Officer may adjust any time limit prescribed or allowed by these rules that
are not otherwise specified by law. The Hearing Officer shall notify all parties of any
determination to change any time limit.
(3) Whenever an expedited time frame is necessary to protect the parties or is in the public interest,
the Hearing Officer may review complaints and conduct the hearing with shorter time frames
as justice requires.

Rule 590-13-6-.03. Complaint Processes
(1) Filing of Complaint
a. All complaints will be accepted by the Secretary of State.
b. Complaints may be submitted online, via hard copy mail, or verbally.
i. For verbal complaints, the Office will document the verbal complaint in writing.
c. Anonymous Complaints
i. Complaints may be submitted anonymously, however, no stay will apply to any charges
associated with an anonymous complaint.
ii. Anonymous complaints will be taken under advisement for purposes of investigations
related to reviewing registration denial, registration suspension, and other actions that
may be taken under O.C.G.A. § 43-17A-3.
(2) Stay of Collection
a. Stays will become effective once the complaint is documented in writing and filed with the
Secretary of State's Office.
(3) Incomplete Complaints
a. If more information about the complaint is needed, the Secretary of State will issue a letter
to the Complainant.
b. Issuance of a letter advising of an incomplete complaint removes the stay on any charges
associated with the complaint.
c. Individuals may resubmit complaints that were incomplete.
(4) Assignment of Hearing Officer
a. Once a complaint is complete, it will be assigned to a Hearing Officer for review. The
Secretary of State will advise the Complainant and the Owners' Association of the
assignment and provide the Hearing Officer's resume.
b. If the Hearing Officer identifies a conflict between themselves and either party, they must
advise the Secretary of State's Office within two (2) business days of appointment in
writing.
c. If a party believes the Hearing Officer has a conflict, they may object to the appointment
in writing in a form as prescribed by the Secretary of State within ten (10) days of the
issuance of the notice of assignment prescribed by the Office of the Secretary of State. The
Secretary of State or designee shall determine if a dispute will be reassigned to another
Hearing Officer in their sole discretion.
(5) Administrative Determinations without Hearing
a. Findings and conclusions may be reached with or without a hearing at the discretion of the
Hearing Officer after investigation, review of documents, and review of the complaint.
b. Findings and conclusions issued without a hearing will be issued in writing and will state
the Hearing Officer's findings, their basis, and any required actions.
c. Any stays will expire upon the date of issuance of the written findings and conclusions
issued without a hearing.

Rule 590-13-6-.04. Pre-Hearing Processes
(1) If a hearing is determined appropriate by the assigned Hearing Officer, the Hearing Officer
shall issue a Notice of Hearing to the complainant and the Owners' Association associated
with complaint, which shall include:
a. the time, place, and nature of the hearing;
b. a short and plain statement of the matters asserted; and
c. the Hearing Officer's resume.
(2) The Secretary of State shall appoint a Clerk to handle administrative portions of the hearing.
All filings made to the Hearing Officer shall be filed with the Clerk and the parties included
on the Notice of Hearing.
(3) Communications with the Hearing Officer by Parties.
a. No party shall communicate with the Hearing Officer regarding the proceeding without
including the other party until it has been resolved, except for administrative, procedural
purposes, or emergencies that do not deal with substantive matters of the case.
b. This rule does not include normal and customary contact between the Secretary of State
and their staff with a Hearing Officer not relating to the contested case.
(4) If multiple individuals have filed complaints regarding the same action or inaction of an
Owners' Association, the Hearing Officer may consolidate their complaints and hold one
hearing to address the issue.

Rule 590-13-7-.01: Notice and Meetings
(1) Notice of a Meeting is fair and reasonable if:
a. The Board of an Owners' Association notifies the Owners of the place, date, and time of
each annual, regular, and special meeting no fewer than seven (7) days nor more than sixty
(60) days before the meeting date;
b. Notice of any meeting must include a description of the matters for which the meeting is
called.
c. Notice is:
i.
Posted in a common area accessible by residents, if applicable; and
ii.
Provided in writing via:
1. Hardcopy to the address of the residents in the Owner's Development unless
requested to be mailed to another address as specified in writing by the Owner; or
2. Electronic means.
(2) Meeting Requirements
a.
Meetings open to all members of an Owners' Association should be scheduled at a time
and place reasonably calculated to permit Owners to attend.
b. Boards are not required to reschedule a member meeting if an Owner has a conflict and the
board has a quorum.
c. Any Owner is permitted to attend open meetings.
d. A Board may not bar an Owner from attending a meeting due to a failure or delay in
payment of charges.

Rule 590-13-7-.02: Good Faith of Owners' Association's Board
(1) Every Board Member shall perform their duties in good faith and with the degree of care an
ordinary, prudent person in a like position would exercise under similar circumstances. Subject
to any other applicable law, requirements include but are not limited to:
a. Making reasonable efforts to ensure Owners and residents are aware of their obligations
while residing in the Owners' Development.
b. Not making materially misleading or false statements to Owners about or related to the
Owners' Association.
c. Ensuring all payments or authorization of payment of charges on behalf of an Owner are
in compliance with the Act and governing documents.
d. Providing written notice, including the method to resolve and challenge, when charging
an Owner specific assessments or other fines or fees.
e. Properly segregating and accounting for an Owners' Association's funds.
f. Enforcing governing documents consistently. If an enforcement action is not consistent, a
documented basis for the differential treatment must be maintained.
g. Disclosing conflicts of interest relating to an Owners' Association's transaction to the other
board members prior to the execution of a contract or payment for goods or services,
whichever occurs first.
h. Ensuring they and their fellow board members do not direct or permit an Agent to engage
in conduct that the Owners' Association could not lawfully undertake directly.
(2) Board members must take reasonable steps to safeguard and maintain records, documents, and
all other assets of an Owners' Association.
a. Owners' Associations must maintain control over their records and documents.
i. Failure of an Agent to remit records and documents shall not alleviate the Owners'
Association of its duty to provide records and documents to a requesting Owner or the
Secretary of State.
b. Board members or Agents acting on their behalf must take reasonable steps to preserve
records, documents, and assets after notice of an election, resignation, termination,
complaint, audit, investigation, litigation or other permissible request to ensure proper
transition and compliance with Act.
(3) Board members must timely transition all Owners' Association's assets to any newly elected
board for the continued function and ongoing compliance of the Owners' Association with the
Act and these rules.
a. Owners' Association records, documents, and administrative credentials are the Owners'
Association's assets, not the personal property of any board member, director, officer,
manager, Agent or vendor.
b. A digital asset or credential to manage such digital asset created, acquired, maintained,
branded, promoted or used primarily to conduct an Owners' Association's business is an
asset of an Owners' Association regardless of who opened the account, whose personal
email address or telephone number was initially used, who paid an initial charge, or whose
name appears as the original administrator.
c. Funds associated with an Owners' Association may not be transitioned or otherwise
comingled with an individual's personal accounts.
d. Intentional deletion or diversion of an Owners' Association's asset is an act of bad faith.
