# Georgia Property Owners' Association Act — Relevant Excerpts
# O.C.G.A. § 44-3-220 through § 44-3-235

*Educational summary prepared for Creek Valley Community Association, Inc. This document is not legal advice. Owners and the Board should consult Georgia counsel for application to specific situations.*

---

## Overview

The Georgia Property Owners' Association Act (the "POAA"), codified at O.C.G.A. § 44-3-220 et seq., is the principal Georgia statute governing residential property owners' associations (HOAs) that have affirmatively elected to be governed by the Act. **Election to be governed by the POAA is not automatic.** A Georgia HOA is subject to the POAA only if its recorded declaration expressly submits the property to the Act under O.C.G.A. § 44-3-222. HOAs that have not so elected are governed primarily by their declaration as a contract among Owners and by the Georgia Nonprofit Corporation Code. Creek Valley's Declaration expressly submits the Property to the POAA.

---

## § 44-3-220 — Short Title

This Code section establishes the short title: "Georgia Property Owners' Association Act."

## § 44-3-221 — Definitions

Provides defined terms used throughout the Act, including "association," "common elements," "declarant," "declaration," "lot," "lot owner," and "property owners' development." The definitions parallel and inform the construction of corresponding terms in association governing documents.

## § 44-3-222 — Submission of Property to the Act

For an association to be governed by the POAA, the recorded declaration must contain an express statement submitting the development to the Act. This is the gateway provision; absent such a statement, the lien-enforcement, foreclosure, and other statutory benefits of the POAA do not apply.

## § 44-3-223 — Declaration Requirements; Bylaws; Rules

Specifies the minimum contents and recording requirements for a valid declaration, including the legal description of the property, the covenants running with the land, the formula for assessment allocation among Lots, voting rights, and the procedures for amendment. This section also authorizes the association to adopt bylaws and rules and regulations and to enforce them through fines, suspension of privileges, and other reasonable remedies, **provided that due process (notice and an opportunity to be heard) is afforded** before adverse action.

## § 44-3-224 — Association as Legal Entity; Powers

Confirms that the property owners' association is a legal entity (typically a Georgia nonprofit corporation) with the power to: hold and dispose of property; enter into contracts; sue and be sued in its own name; levy assessments; adopt and amend bylaws and rules; impose reasonable charges for late payment, for returned checks, and for the reasonable use of common elements; and exercise all powers necessary or incidental to its purposes consistent with the declaration and the Nonprofit Corporation Code.

## § 44-3-225 — Assessments; Budget and Notice; Increases

Authorizes the association to levy regular and special assessments against Lot Owners in accordance with the formula set forth in the declaration. The board must prepare an annual budget and provide notice of the budget and assessments to Owners in advance of the fiscal year. Material increases in regular assessments and the levy of certain special assessments may require Owner notice or approval as provided in the declaration; **the POAA does not itself impose a hard cap on assessment increases**, but most declarations (including Creek Valley's, § 7.1) do.

## § 44-3-226 — Amendment of Declaration

Governs the procedure by which a declaration may be amended. Typically requires the affirmative vote or written consent of Owners holding not less than two-thirds (2/3) of the total voting power, unless the declaration specifies a different threshold. Amendments must be recorded in the county records to be effective against subsequent purchasers. Certain amendments — such as those affecting access to a Lot or fundamentally altering the assessment formula — may require unanimous consent of affected Owners.

## § 44-3-227 — Common Elements; Use and Allocation

Addresses ownership, use, and allocation of common elements (referred to as "Common Area" in the Creek Valley Declaration). Common elements may be owned by the association or held in undivided interest by the Owners, depending on the structure set forth in the declaration. The board has authority to adopt reasonable rules governing use of common elements, including hours, guest policies, and amenity fees.

## § 44-3-228 — Insurance Requirements

Provides for property and liability insurance to be maintained by the association on the common elements. The board has fiduciary responsibility to maintain coverage in amounts adequate for full replacement of common elements and to protect against liability arising from association operations. Owners typically remain responsible for insuring their individual Lots and Residences. **Best practice** is for the association to also maintain Directors and Officers (D&O) liability insurance and a fidelity bond, although these are required by the declaration/bylaws rather than the POAA itself.

## § 44-3-229 — Indemnification

Authorizes the association to indemnify directors, officers, and committee members against expenses incurred in defending actions brought by reason of their service, consistent with the indemnification provisions of the Georgia Nonprofit Corporation Code. Indemnification is generally available only where the indemnified person acted in good faith and in a manner reasonably believed to be in the best interests of the association.

## § 44-3-230 — Meetings; Voting; Proxies

Governs meetings of the membership, including notice, quorum, voting (in person, by proxy, or by written/electronic ballot where authorized), and the conduct of elections. Proxies are limited in duration (generally not more than eleven months unless otherwise specified) and must be in writing. Recent amendments have clarified the use of **electronic voting and remote-meeting participation** by both the membership and the board.

## § 44-3-231 — Records; Inspection by Owners

Guarantees Lot Owners the right to inspect and copy the books and records of the association during regular business hours at a reasonable location, upon written request stating a proper purpose. Inspectable records include the declaration, bylaws, rules, minutes of meetings, financial records, contracts, and the membership roster. The association may charge a reasonable copying fee. Certain records are exempt from inspection, including: personnel records; attorney-client privileged communications; pending litigation files; and records of covenant-enforcement actions concerning specific Owners other than the requesting Owner.

## § 44-3-232 — Assessment Lien; Continuing Lien; Priority

**The cornerstone enforcement provision.** Unpaid assessments, together with interest, late charges, attorney's fees, and costs of collection, constitute a **continuing lien on the Lot** from the date the assessment becomes due. The lien is automatic — it arises by operation of the recorded declaration and this statute, without the need to record a separate lien instrument — although recording a notice of lien is the customary practice prior to foreclosure. The assessment lien has **priority over all other liens except**: (i) liens for ad valorem taxes; (ii) the lien of a first mortgage of record; and (iii) certain other governmental liens. The lien continues against the Lot regardless of transfer of title, and a purchaser takes subject to outstanding amounts.

## § 44-3-232.1 — Foreclosure of Assessment Lien

Provides the procedure for **judicial or non-judicial foreclosure** of the assessment lien, generally following the same procedures applicable to foreclosure of mortgages under Georgia law. Before foreclosure, the association must provide statutorily required notices to the Owner and any junior lienholders. Recent practice and case law emphasize the importance of strict compliance with notice and advertisement requirements; defective notice is a frequent ground for setting aside HOA foreclosures.

## § 44-3-233 — Disclosure to Purchasers; Closing Statements

Requires the association, upon written request and payment of a reasonable fee, to furnish to a prospective purchaser (or the closing agent) a statement of: (i) the amount of any unpaid assessments, late charges, and other amounts owed on the Lot; (ii) a copy of the declaration, bylaws, and current rules; (iii) the current operating budget; and (iv) a description of any pending litigation involving the association. The statement is generally binding on the association as to the amounts owed as of the date of issuance. Typical statutory turnaround is **ten (10) business days**. Reasonable fees for closing letters and resale certificates are permitted.

## § 44-3-234 — Statutory Construction; Severability

Provides rules of construction: the Act is to be liberally construed to effectuate its purposes; provisions are severable; and where the declaration and the Act conflict on a matter as to which the Act is mandatory, the Act controls.

## § 44-3-235 — Application to Pre-Existing Associations

Addresses application of the POAA to associations existing before the Act's effective date, generally requiring affirmative election (via amendment of the declaration) to be governed by the POAA. Pre-existing associations that have not elected to be governed by the POAA remain bound by their declarations and the general law of restrictive covenants.

## Related Georgia Statutes Frequently Cited

- **O.C.G.A. § 44-5-60** — Duration of restrictive covenants; general law applicable to non-POAA associations. Covenants automatically renew in 20-year cycles unless a majority of Owners object.
- **O.C.G.A. § 44-5-60.1** — Political signs; protects Owner right to display political signs subject to reasonable time, place, and manner restrictions.
- **O.C.G.A. § 14-3-101 et seq.** — Georgia Nonprofit Corporation Code, applicable to HOAs organized as nonprofit corporations.
- **O.C.G.A. § 14-3-830** — General standard of conduct for directors.
- **O.C.G.A. § 14-3-1602** — Member inspection rights under the Nonprofit Corporation Code.
- **O.C.G.A. § 44-14-160 et seq.** — Foreclosure procedures.

## Owner Rights — Practical Summary

Under the POAA and related Georgia law, each Lot Owner generally has the right to:

1. **Vote** on matters submitted to the membership, in proportion to the voting rights allocated by the declaration.
2. **Run for the Board** and serve in office, subject to qualifications set in the bylaws.
3. **Attend regular and special meetings** of the membership and the open portions of board meetings.
4. **Inspect records** of the association upon written request for a proper purpose.
5. **Receive notice** of meetings, budgets, assessment increases, and proposed amendments to the declaration.
6. **Receive due process** — notice and an opportunity to be heard — before fines, suspension of privileges, or other adverse enforcement action.
7. **Receive a closing/resale statement** prior to sale.
8. **Display political signs** within reasonable size and timing limits.
9. **Install satellite dishes** of one meter or less under the FCC OTARD rule.

## Board Duties — Practical Summary

The Board of Directors, collectively and individually, has duties including:

1. **Fiduciary duty** to act in good faith and in the best interests of the association.
2. **Duty of care** — exercising the care of an ordinarily prudent person in a like position.
3. **Duty of loyalty** — avoiding conflicts of interest and self-dealing.
4. **Maintenance** of common elements in good repair, including funding adequate reserves.
5. **Insurance** of the association and common elements at appropriate levels.
6. **Enforcement** of the declaration, bylaws, and rules in a consistent, non-discriminatory manner, with due process.
7. **Financial stewardship** — preparing budgets, collecting assessments, maintaining accurate records, and arranging audits or reviews as required.
8. **Transparency** — providing required notices, holding open meetings (with limited executive-session exceptions), and responding to legitimate Owner inquiries.

## Accommodation Requirements (Federal Overlay)

While not part of the POAA itself, the federal **Fair Housing Act (42 U.S.C. § 3601 et seq.)** and the **Americans with Disabilities Act** (where applicable to common areas open to the public) require associations to provide **reasonable accommodations** for Owners and occupants with disabilities. Common examples include: permitting assistance/service animals notwithstanding pet restrictions; allowing wheelchair ramps and similar modifications notwithstanding architectural standards; and providing accessible parking. Associations may not charge fees for reasonable accommodations but may require reasonable documentation of disability-related need where the disability is not obvious.
