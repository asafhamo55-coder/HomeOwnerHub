## Article I — Definitions

### Section 1.1 Defined Terms

For purposes of this Declaration, the following terms have the meanings set forth below:

- **"Association"** means Madison Park Homeowners Association, Inc., a Georgia nonprofit corporation.
- **"Board"** means the Board of Directors of the Association.
- **"Common Area"** means all real property owned by the Association for the common use and enjoyment of Members, including without limitation entry monuments, the swimming pool, the cabana, internal walking paths, the retention pond, and landscape easements.
- **"Lot"** means any individually numbered residential lot as shown on the recorded subdivision plat.
- **"Member"** means every Owner of a Lot.
- **"Owner"** means the record holder of fee simple title to a Lot.
- **"ARC"** means the Architectural Review Committee appointed by the Board.
- **"Annual Assessment"** means the recurring assessment levied per Section 6.1.
- **"Special Assessment"** means an extraordinary assessment levied per Section 6.3.
- **"Cure Period"** means the period during which an Owner may remedy a violation before further enforcement action is taken, as defined per violation in Section 7.2.

### Section 1.2 Statutory Framework

This Declaration is governed by the Georgia Property Owners' Association Act, O.C.G.A. § 44-3-220 *et seq.* (the "Act"). Where any provision of this Declaration conflicts with the Act, the Act controls.

## Article II — The Association

### Section 2.1 Membership

Every Owner is automatically a Member of the Association. Membership is appurtenant to and may not be separated from ownership of a Lot.

### Section 2.2 Voting Rights

Each Lot is entitled to one (1) vote. Where a Lot is owned by more than one person, the co-Owners must designate in writing which co-Owner will exercise the vote. In the absence of designation, no vote may be cast for that Lot.

## Article III — Board of Directors

### Section 3.1 Composition

The Board consists of five (5) Directors elected by the Members. Directors serve staggered two-year terms; in odd-numbered years three (3) seats stand for election, and in even-numbered years two (2) seats stand for election.

### Section 3.2 Powers

The Board has all powers necessary for the administration of the Association's affairs, including without limitation: levying assessments, promulgating Rules and Regulations under Section 13, contracting with vendors and managing agents, enforcing the Declaration, and conducting hearings under Section 7.3.

### Section 3.3 Meetings

Regular Board meetings are held no less than quarterly. The schedule of regular meetings is posted on the Association's website. Notice of each meeting is delivered to Members no fewer than seven (7) days in advance, except in the case of an emergency meeting which may be called on twenty-four (24) hours' notice.

## Article IV — Architectural Control

### Section 4.1 Approval Required

No exterior modification, addition, or alteration to any Lot — including but not limited to paint color, roofing material, landscaping above twenty-four inches (24") in height, fences, satellite dishes, accessory structures, sheds, swimming pools, decks, patios, and exterior lighting — may be commenced without the prior written approval of the Architectural Review Committee.

### Section 4.2 Submission Procedure

ARC submissions must be in writing on the form provided by the Association and must include drawings to scale, dimensions, material samples where applicable, and the proposed start date. The ARC will respond within thirty (30) days of receipt of a complete submission. Failure of the ARC to respond within thirty (30) days constitutes approval.

### Section 4.3 Approved Color Palette

Approved exterior paint colors are limited to those on the Association's Approved Color Palette, available from the Managing Agent. Front doors may be painted in any color from the Approved Door Color Palette without prior ARC review, provided the Owner notifies the Managing Agent within thirty (30) days of painting. Front doors painted in a color not on the Approved Door Color Palette require prior ARC approval per Section 4.1.

### Section 4.4 Solar Energy Devices

Pursuant to O.C.G.A. § 44-3-243, the installation of solar energy devices is permitted on any Lot. The ARC may impose reasonable restrictions on the placement of such devices to minimize visual impact from the street, but may not prohibit installation outright or impose restrictions that significantly increase the cost of the device or significantly decrease its efficiency. ARC review is limited to placement and screening, not the decision to install.

### Section 4.5 Satellite Dishes and Antennas

Consistent with the FCC Over-the-Air Reception Devices Rule (47 C.F.R. § 1.4000), satellite dishes one (1) meter or less in diameter and television antennas may be installed on any Owner's Lot without ARC approval, provided that the device is placed in the least visible location consistent with acceptable signal reception. The ARC may not require relocation of a device that meaningfully degrades reception.

### Section 4.6 Fences

Fences are permitted only with prior ARC approval. Approved materials are wood (cedar or pressure-treated pine, painted or stained) and aluminum or steel in a black or bronze finish. Chain-link, vinyl, and PVC fences are prohibited. Maximum height is six feet (6'). No fence may be installed in front of the front building line of the residence.

### Section 4.7 Sheds and Outbuildings

Detached sheds and outbuildings are permitted only with ARC approval and only when (a) located behind the rear building line of the residence, (b) fully screened from the street by approved landscaping, and (c) no larger than one hundred twenty (120) square feet. Color and material must match the residence.

### Section 4.8 Landscaping

Landscaping changes that involve hardscape (e.g., walkways, retaining walls, water features), removal of trees with a trunk diameter exceeding six inches (6") at breast height, or installation of any plant material exceeding twenty-four inches (24") in mature height require ARC approval. Routine maintenance, mulching, and seasonal annual flower bed changes do not.

### Section 4.9 Appeal Procedure

An Owner whose ARC submission is denied may appeal in writing to the Board within thirty (30) days of the denial. The Board will hear the appeal at its next regularly scheduled meeting and will issue a written decision within fourteen (14) days of the hearing. The Board's decision is final.

## Article V — Use Restrictions

### Section 5.1 Residential Use

Each Lot is restricted to single-family residential use. No Lot may be used for any commercial, industrial, or other non-residential purpose, except as expressly permitted by Section 5.2.

### Section 5.2 Home-Based Business

A home-based business is permitted on a Lot only if all of the following are met: (a) the business has no on-premises employees other than the Owner or family members residing on the Lot; (b) no customers, clients, or patrons visit the Lot for business purposes; (c) no signage, advertising, or external evidence of the business is visible from outside the residence; and (d) the business does not generate vehicle traffic, noise, or odors that constitute a nuisance under Section 5.11. Home daycares, nail or hair salons, repair shops, retail businesses, and any business requiring customer visits are expressly prohibited.

### Section 5.3 Pets

No more than two (2) domesticated household pets per Lot are permitted. Permitted pets are limited to dogs, cats, and small caged birds. All dogs must be leashed when outside the residence and Lot. Pet waste must be cleaned up immediately by the Owner. Aggressive breeds as enumerated in Rules and Regulations Section 13.1 are prohibited. Livestock, poultry, exotic animals, and reptiles other than small terrarium-housed lizards are prohibited.

### Section 5.4 Vehicles and Parking

No commercial vehicle (defined as any vehicle bearing exterior business signage or rated for more than one ton), recreational vehicle, motor home, boat, trailer, jet-ski, or inoperable vehicle may be parked on any street, driveway, or Lot for more than forty-eight (48) hours, except when fully enclosed within a garage. Resident vehicles must be parked in the Owner's driveway or garage. Street parking is reserved for guests and is limited to seventy-two (72) consecutive hours; vehicles parked on the street for longer than seventy-two (72) hours may be towed at the Owner's expense.

### Section 5.5 Leasing

Leasing of a Lot is permitted only under a written lease with a minimum initial term of twelve (12) months. Short-term rentals — including but not limited to Airbnb, VRBO, HomeAway, and any rental for a term of less than thirty (30) days — are expressly prohibited. The Owner remains responsible for the conduct of the tenant and for ensuring that the tenant complies with this Declaration and the Rules and Regulations. A copy of the executed lease must be provided to the Managing Agent within ten (10) days of execution.

### Section 5.6 Signs

Only the following signs are permitted on a Lot, without ARC approval:
- One (1) "For Sale" or "For Rent" sign not exceeding four (4) square feet, professionally produced
- Political signs not exceeding six (6) square feet, displayed no more than thirty (30) days before an election and removed no later than seven (7) days after the election
- Security company signs not exceeding one (1) square foot

All other signs — including commercial signs, contractor signs (except during active work, removed within seven (7) days of work completion), and decorative signs visible from the street — require prior ARC approval.

### Section 5.7 Flags

Pursuant to O.C.G.A. § 44-3-241, the display of the flag of the United States, the State of Georgia, the official flag of any branch of the United States Armed Forces, and the flag of the State of Georgia is permitted on any Lot without prior approval, subject to reasonable restrictions on size and method of display. The HOA flag and seasonal decorative flags not exceeding three feet by five feet (3' × 5') are permitted without approval. All other flags require prior ARC approval.

### Section 5.8 Holiday Decorations

Winter holiday decorations (including but not limited to lights, wreaths, and lawn displays) are permitted from November 1 through January 15 and must be removed no later than January 15. Decorations for other holidays are permitted from fourteen (14) days before the holiday through seven (7) days after. Decorations displayed outside these windows constitute a violation.

### Section 5.9 Trash and Recycling

Trash and recycling containers must be stored within the Owner's garage or behind the rear building line of the residence, screened from view from the street. Containers may be placed at the curb no earlier than twelve (12) hours before the scheduled pickup time and must be returned to their stored location within twelve (12) hours after pickup.

### Section 5.10 Exterior Storage

No items — including but not limited to lawn equipment, ladders, building materials, firewood (except seasoned firewood neatly stacked in a side or rear yard), toys, sporting equipment, or vehicle parts — may be stored on any Lot in a location visible from any street.

### Section 5.11 Nuisance

No noxious, illegal, or offensive activity may be conducted on any Lot or in any Common Area. Owners must conduct themselves so as not to unreasonably disturb the peace, quiet, comfort, or enjoyment of other Owners. Outdoor noise audible at the property line is restricted during the quiet hours specified in Rules and Regulations Section 13.2.

## Article VI — Assessments

### Section 6.1 Annual Assessment

Annual Assessments fund the operating budget approved by the Board. Annual Assessments are payable in equal monthly installments due on the first (1st) day of each month. The Board fixes the amount of the Annual Assessment for each fiscal year no later than thirty (30) days before the start of the fiscal year and provides written notice to all Members.

### Section 6.2 Late Fees

A late fee of five percent (5%) of the unpaid balance accrues on the fifteenth (15th) day of each month if the monthly installment remains unpaid. Late fees are themselves Assessments and are subject to lien rights under Section 6.4.

### Section 6.3 Special Assessments

The Board may levy a Special Assessment for any extraordinary expense not anticipated in the operating budget. A Special Assessment that, when aggregated with all other Special Assessments levied within the same fiscal year, exceeds twenty percent (20%) of the Annual Assessment requires the affirmative vote of a majority of Members present at a meeting at which a quorum (Section 12.2) is achieved.

### Section 6.4 Lien Rights

Pursuant to O.C.G.A. § 44-3-232, the Association has a statutory lien against any Lot whose Assessments remain unpaid for more than sixty (60) days. The Association must send a written demand letter to the Owner at least thirty (30) days before recording the lien. The lien attaches as of the date of recording and is prior to all liens except the lien of a first mortgage of record and the lien for taxes.

### Section 6.5 Foreclosure

The Association may foreclose its lien by judicial proceeding pursuant to O.C.G.A. § 44-3-232. The Association may not non-judicially foreclose. Foreclosure proceedings may not commence until the Assessment delinquency exceeds one thousand dollars ($1,000) or has remained unpaid for more than one (1) year, whichever occurs first.

### Section 6.6 Attorney Fees and Costs

Pursuant to O.C.G.A. § 44-3-232, the Association is entitled to recover from the delinquent Owner all reasonable attorney fees, court costs, and other costs of collection incurred in enforcing this Article VI. Such costs are themselves Assessments and are subject to lien rights.

### Section 6.7 Application of Payments

All payments received from an Owner are applied in the following order: first, to attorney fees and costs of collection; second, to late fees; third, to fines; and fourth, to outstanding Assessments, oldest first. An Owner may not direct the application of payment to any specific charge.

## Article VII — Fines and Enforcement

### Section 7.1 Fine Schedule

The default fine schedule is as follows, subject to modification by the Board through the Rules and Regulations:
- **Minor violation** (e.g., trash containers visible, holiday decorations not timely removed): twenty-five dollars ($25)
- **Moderate violation** (e.g., parking violation, pet leash violation, sign violation): fifty dollars ($50)
- **Major violation** (e.g., unauthorized exterior modification, repeated violations, leasing violations, prohibited pet breeds): one hundred dollars ($100)

### Section 7.2 Notice and Cure Period

Before a fine is assessed, the Owner is entitled to written notice of the alleged violation and a cure period of fourteen (14) days to remedy the violation, except where this Declaration or the Rules and Regulations specify a different cure period. Notice must include: (a) the specific provision alleged to be violated; (b) the factual basis for the alleged violation; (c) the cure period; and (d) the consequences of failure to cure, including the right to a hearing under Section 7.3.

### Section 7.3 Hearing Rights

Pursuant to O.C.G.A. § 44-3-223, an Owner who has received notice of an alleged violation may request a hearing before the Board by written request delivered to the Managing Agent within ten (10) days of receipt of notice. The hearing must be held within thirty (30) days of the request, and the Board must issue a written decision within fourteen (14) days of the hearing. Until the hearing has concluded and a decision has been issued, no fine may be imposed.

### Section 7.4 Continuing Violations

If a violation is not cured within the applicable cure period, the Board may assess a continuing fine of up to twenty-five dollars ($25) per day for each day the violation continues, up to a maximum aggregate fine of one thousand dollars ($1,000) per violation.

### Section 7.5 Suspension of Privileges

The Board may suspend an Owner's right to use the Common Area amenities (including the swimming pool and cabana) and the Owner's right to vote at any Association meeting if the Owner is more than sixty (60) days delinquent in payment of Assessments or has an unresolved violation that has remained un-cured for more than thirty (30) days.

## Article VIII — Insurance

### Section 8.1 Association Coverage

The Association maintains property insurance covering the Common Area improvements at full replacement cost; commercial general liability insurance with limits of not less than one million dollars ($1,000,000) per occurrence and two million dollars ($2,000,000) aggregate; and directors and officers liability insurance with limits of not less than one million dollars ($1,000,000).

### Section 8.2 Owner Coverage

Each Owner is responsible for maintaining a homeowner's insurance policy covering the Owner's residence and all improvements within the Lot, with coverage at full replacement cost. Owners who lease their Lot must maintain an HO-6 (or substantially equivalent) policy at minimum, covering interior improvements, personal property, and loss of use. Each Owner is also responsible for obtaining personal liability coverage with limits of not less than three hundred thousand dollars ($300,000).

### Section 8.3 Deductible Allocation

In the event of a loss covered under both Association and Owner policies, the Association's deductible is funded from the operating budget. Where damage to a Lot or its residence is caused by negligence of the Owner or an occupant of the Owner's Lot, the Owner is responsible for the Association's deductible up to the amount of the deductible.

### Section 8.4 Vendor Insurance

All vendors performing work on Common Area must provide proof of commercial general liability insurance with limits of not less than one million dollars ($1,000,000) per occurrence, workers' compensation insurance as required by Georgia law, and where the vendor will operate motor vehicles on Association property, automobile liability insurance with limits of not less than one million dollars ($1,000,000). The Association must be named as additional insured.

## Article IX — Maintenance

### Section 9.1 Association Responsibilities

The Association maintains, repairs, and replaces the Common Area, including without limitation: the swimming pool and cabana, entry monuments and signage, the retention pond, irrigation systems serving Common Area, walking paths, perimeter landscaping along external roadways, mailbox kiosks (where the Association has installed shared mailboxes), and the entry gate (if any).

### Section 9.2 Owner Responsibilities

Each Owner maintains, repairs, and replaces all portions of the Owner's Lot and residence, including without limitation: the residence structure, the roof, exterior paint, gutters, the driveway, walkways within the Lot, mailbox post (where individual mailboxes are used), landscaping within the Lot, irrigation serving the Lot, fencing, and any approved structures.

### Section 9.3 Standard of Maintenance

All Lots and residences must be maintained in a neat, attractive, and sanitary condition consistent with the general character of the community. Where the Association determines that an Owner has failed to meet this standard and the Owner has not cured the failure within the cure period under Section 7.2, the Association may, after a Section 7.3 hearing, enter the Lot to perform the necessary maintenance and assess the cost back to the Owner as an Assessment.

## Article X — Common Areas and Amenities

### Section 10.1 Easement of Use

Every Owner has a non-exclusive easement of use and enjoyment in the Common Area, subject to the rules of use established by the Board.

### Section 10.2 Pool and Cabana

The swimming pool is open from Memorial Day through Labor Day, daily from 6:00 a.m. to 10:00 p.m. The cabana is available for reservation by Members in good standing at no charge, subject to scheduling availability through the Managing Agent.

### Section 10.3 Guest Limits

Each Owner may have up to four (4) guests at any single time at the swimming pool. Guests must be accompanied by the Owner or by a household resident at all times.

## Article XI — Amendment Procedure

### Section 11.1 Vote Required

This Declaration may be amended only by the affirmative vote of two-thirds (2/3) of the total Membership of the Association. Each Lot is entitled to one (1) vote (Section 2.2).

### Section 11.2 Recording

Any amendment must be reduced to writing, executed by the President and Secretary of the Association, and recorded in the Office of the Clerk of Superior Court of Fulton County, Georgia. An amendment is effective only upon recording.

## Article XII — Bylaws Summary

> *The following provisions are summarized from the Association's Bylaws and are incorporated herein by reference. In the event of any conflict between this summary and the Bylaws as recorded, the recorded Bylaws control.*

### Section 12.1 Annual Meeting

The Annual Meeting of Members is held in October of each year on a date set by the Board, with written notice to all Members no fewer than fourteen (14) days in advance. The meeting may be held in person, virtually, or in a hybrid format at the Board's discretion.

### Section 12.2 Quorum

The presence of Members holding twenty-five percent (25%) of the total votes of the Association, in person or by proxy, constitutes a quorum at any Members' meeting. If a quorum is not present, the meeting may be adjourned and reconvened with the same notice procedure, at which the quorum requirement is reduced to ten percent (10%).

### Section 12.3 Board Composition and Terms

The Board consists of five (5) Directors elected by the Members, serving staggered two-year terms (Section 3.1). Directors must be Members in good standing at the time of election and throughout their term.

### Section 12.4 Officer Roles

The Board elects from among its members a President, Vice President, Treasurer, and Secretary. The President presides at meetings and has general supervisory authority. The Treasurer oversees the Association's finances and signs checks (which must be co-signed by one other officer for amounts exceeding two thousand five hundred dollars ($2,500)). The Secretary maintains the minutes and records.

## Article XIII — Rules and Regulations

> *The Board has authority under Section 3.2 to promulgate Rules and Regulations that elaborate on this Declaration. The current Rules and Regulations are summarized below; the official Rules and Regulations are maintained by the Managing Agent.*

### Section 13.1 Prohibited Dog Breeds

The following dog breeds and any mix thereof are prohibited on any Lot or in any Common Area: Pit Bull (American Pit Bull Terrier, American Staffordshire Terrier, Staffordshire Bull Terrier), Rottweiler, Doberman Pinscher, Chow Chow, Akita, Wolf Hybrid, and Presa Canario. Owners with a prohibited breed registered with the Association as of the effective date of this Rule may keep that specific dog for the remainder of its natural life, subject to leash and waste rules under Section 5.3.

### Section 13.2 Quiet Hours

Quiet hours during which outdoor noise audible at the property line is restricted are: 10:00 p.m. to 7:00 a.m. Sunday night through Friday morning, and 11:00 p.m. to 8:00 a.m. on Friday night and Saturday night. Quiet hours apply to amplified music, construction, lawn equipment, and other sources of audible noise.

### Section 13.3 Pool Guest Sign-In

Each Member must sign in their guests at the pool entry kiosk before each use of the pool. Pool passes (issued by the Managing Agent) must be displayed on demand. The Association is not responsible for accidents involving unsigned-in guests.

### Section 13.4 Architectural Standards Manual

The detailed Architectural Standards Manual maintained by the ARC contains the Approved Color Palette (Section 4.3), approved fence designs (Section 4.6), and approved shed designs (Section 4.7). The Manual is available from the Managing Agent at no charge to Members.

## Article XIV — General Provisions

### Section 14.1 Severability

If any provision of this Declaration is held invalid, the remaining provisions remain in full force and effect.

### Section 14.2 Headings

Article and Section headings are for convenience of reference only and have no substantive legal effect.

### Section 14.3 Order of Conflict

In the event of any conflict between or among the governing instruments, the order of authority from highest to lowest is: (1) the Georgia Property Owners' Association Act and other applicable Georgia statutes; (2) this Declaration; (3) the Articles of Incorporation; (4) the Bylaws; and (5) the Rules and Regulations.

### Section 14.4 Notice

Any notice required to be given to an Owner under this Declaration may be given by United States mail to the address shown on the Association's records, by hand delivery, or by electronic mail to the address the Owner has provided to the Managing Agent in writing.

### Section 14.5 Term

This Declaration runs with the land in perpetuity, subject to amendment under Article XI.
