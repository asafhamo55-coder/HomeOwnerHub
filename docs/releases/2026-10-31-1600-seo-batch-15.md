## Summary

The website has three more pages for boards. A Georgia template covers the certified board resolution a bank, lender or court can rely on. A Georgia guide explains what a board can borrow, sue over or amend, and what needs owner votes. A Texas guide covers flag display. Law quotes come only from the checked official text, each page carries a not-legal-advice note and ends with "Book a demo", and each says plainly what HomeownerHub does and does not do.

## What's new

### New pages on www.homeownerledger.online
- `/templates/georgia-hoa-board-resolution-template`: a checklist and a fill-in resolution with fields for the association, meeting date, quorum, motion text, vote count and the secretary's certification, built on O.C.G.A. § 44-3-231(b).
- `/guides/georgia-hoa-board-borrowing-and-lawsuits`: the borrowing, litigation and amendment powers in §§ 44-3-231 and 44-3-226, the board quorum in § 44-3-228, and where the board needs owners.
- `/guides/texas-hoa-flag-display-rules`: Tex. Prop. Code § 202.012. Which flags an association cannot ban, what it can still regulate, and the flagpole rule.

The pages say HomeownerHub keeps the meeting record: the agenda builder, the quorum kit, the motion log and minutes approval. It does not draft or certify a resolution, and it does not judge a flag rule.

The new pages are listed on the Guides and Templates pages, in the search palette (Cmd-K or Ctrl-K) and in the sitemap. The Texas page is also on the Texas card. The board powers guide now links to the two Georgia pages.
