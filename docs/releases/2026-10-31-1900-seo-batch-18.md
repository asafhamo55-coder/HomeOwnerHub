## Summary

The website has three more state-law guides for boards: Florida elections and quorum, California election rules, and Florida member meetings with proxies. Law quotes come only from the checked official text, each page carries a not-legal-advice note and ends with "Book a demo", and each says plainly what HomeownerHub does and does not do.

## What's new

### New pages on www.homeownerledger.online
- `/guides/florida-hoa-elections-and-quorum`: what Fla. Stat. § 720.306 says about the 30 percent quorum default, the 14-day meeting notice, self-nomination, absentee secret ballots, the plurality winner, the bar on delinquent candidates and the 60-day challenge window. It says plainly that the section sets no separate candidate notice period and no inspector. It points to the election planner and the meeting quorum kit.
- `/guides/california-hoa-election-rules`: what Civil Code §§ 5100 to 5145 say about secret ballots, nominations, the independent inspector of elections, and why unpaid assessments cannot cost a member the ballot (§ 5105(h)(1)). Every statement is taken from quoted text. It points to the election planner and its California inspector record.
- `/guides/florida-hoa-member-meetings-proxies-and-recording`: special meetings, the 3-minute speaking floor, adjournment, what makes a proxy valid and recording, from Fla. Stat. § 720.306. It points to the quorum kit and its proxy form.

The pages say HomeownerHub plans the election dates, prints ballots and tally sheets, and counts quorum and proxies. It does not hold your bylaws, send notices, appoint an inspector or run an online vote.

The new pages are listed on the Guides page, in the search palette (Cmd-K or Ctrl-K), in the sitemap and on the Florida and California state pages.

## Under the hood

- Every new law quote is checked word for word against the official text.
- The Florida meeting notice guide and the director certification checklist now link to the elections guide.
