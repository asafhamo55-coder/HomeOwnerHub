## Summary

Texas and Georgia boards can now prepare the paperwork a home sale needs, with the same safety checks Florida and California already have. The app can load California and Texas HOA law from the official state sources, and it never answers law questions from memory. Boards also get an owner roster they can safely hand to an owner who asks for records.

## What's new

### Home sales
- **Texas resale certificate** (Property Code § 207.003): due 10 business days after the request, fee capped at $375, and free if late. The app fills in the assessment, the amount owed and open violations. Every other required item is a blank you must fill before printing.
- **Georgia statement of amounts due** (O.C.G.A. § 44-3-232(d)): due in 5 business days. The fee is $10 at most, and only if your declaration allows one. A warning explains that missing the deadline loses the lien against the buyer.

### State law
- **California and Texas law** can now be loaded from the official state sources: 234 sections of the Davis-Stirling Act and 61 sections of Texas Property Code chapters 209 and 202. Florida loads 36 sections. Production loads them after a one-time admin step (owner action OA-9).
- **Never from memory:** if a state's law isn't loaded yet, Ask about state law and the resident assistant say so plainly instead of guessing.

### Records requests
- **Owner roster for records requests:** a copy of the owner list you can give an owner who asks. It shows names and addresses only, never phones, shares or balances. Email addresses follow each state's rule (for example, Florida never includes them). Every export is recorded.

### Messages
- **Drafts tab:** now also lists inbox replies that are waiting for you, next to unsent announcements.

### Other
- **Terms of Service** (marketing site): the description now says HOA management only. Nothing else in the terms changed.
- **Tests** confirm that a retried violation draft from the walk-the-community queue is counted only once.
