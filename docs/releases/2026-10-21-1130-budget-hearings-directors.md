## Summary

Budget season tools are here. A checklist walks the board through adopting next year's budget lawfully, and the dashboard shows the next step. In Florida and California, a fine now requires the hearing steps the law requires. Florida boards can also track the education certificate every new director must file within 90 days.

## What's new

### Budget adoption kit
- **Adopt this budget:** a checklist on each draft or approved budget. It covers scheduling the adoption meeting, sending its notice, approving and recording the adoption, sending owners the budget, and then setting the new dues.
- **Florida:** it checks the notice deadlines (7 days by mail or 48 hours by posting; 14 days, mailed and posted, if special assessments are on the agenda). It also warns if the agenda doesn't say assessments will be considered.
- **California:** it counts down the 30–90-day window for sending owners the budget. For a calendar-year association, that runs October 2 to December 1.
- **Budget season card:** the dashboard shows your next step.

### Fine hearings are now required (Florida and California)
- **What's required:** a violation can only be moved to "Fined" once the hearing record shows the notice sent in time, the hearing held, and the outcome recorded. In Florida the panel must have at least three people who aren't on the board.
- **Panel check:** if a panel member's name matches a board member, the app flags it. It can't check relatives, and the page says so.
- **Override:** "Attorney advised" is the only override, and it's recorded.
- **Morning email:** it now lists hearing requests waiting to be scheduled, notices due, and findings due.

### Florida director certification
- **Tracking:** each board member's 90-day deadline to file their education certificate after being elected (Fla. Stat. § 720.3033), plus the 4-year renewal.
- **Where you see it:** on Members, the board handover page, the dashboard and the morning email. "Mark certified" records it.

## Good to know
- If you have a Florida or California violation ready to fine, record its hearing first, or use the attorney-advised override.
- Texas and Georgia fines work as before.
