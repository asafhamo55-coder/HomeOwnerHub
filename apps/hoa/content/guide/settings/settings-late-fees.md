Use this to decide whether HomeownerHub adds a late fee to charges that stay unpaid, and how much.

## Before you start
- Board members and admins can change this.
- Until the board saves a policy here, no late fee is charged on any charge.
- Have your declaration or bylaws to hand. You need the article and section that allows a late charge.

## Steps
1. Open **Settings**, then the **Late fees** tab.
2. Turn on **Charge late fees**.
3. Under **Late fee amount**, choose **Percent of the late amount** or **Flat amount**, and enter it.
4. Enter the **Grace period (days)**: how many days after the due date a charge becomes late.
5. Leave **Exclude fines** ticked unless your declaration allows a late fee on fines.
6. Tick **Our declaration or bylaws provide for a late charge**, and enter where it says so under **Where it says so**.
7. Select [[Save late-fee policy]].

## What happens next
Each night, a charge still unpaid after its due date and the grace period gets one late fee, posted to your books as late fee income. Each charge gets one late fee at most. A late fee never gets its own late fee, and collection costs and lien fees never get one.

A percent is taken of the amount still unpaid on the charge. The board's morning email says how many late fees were charged overnight and the total.

## If something goes wrong
- "Check the highlighted fields.": a field is missing or above your state's limit. The message under the field says which. Fix it and save again.
- **Your saved late-fee policy isn’t in use**: it was changed outside this screen, so no late fees are being charged. Check it and save again.
- "Couldn’t save the late-fee policy. Try again.": nothing changed. Save again.

## Good to know
- HomeownerHub enforces your state's limit when you save and again each night. **Georgia:** a late charge is allowed only if your declaration provides for one, up to $10 or 10% of the late amount, whichever is greater (O.C.G.A. § 44-3-232(b)(1)). A Georgia condominium association has the same limit under the Georgia Condominium Act, if its condominium instruments provide for a late charge (O.C.G.A. § 44-3-109(b)(1)).
- **Florida:** a late charge only if the declaration or bylaws provide for one, up to $25 or 5%, whichever is greater (Fla. Stat. § 720.3085(3)(a)).
- **California:** up to $10 or 10%, whichever is greater, and not before 15 days after the due date (Cal. Civ. Code § 5650(b)).
- **Other states, including Texas:** HomeownerHub has no rule loaded. Check your governing documents. No limit applies beyond what the board sets.
- A percent above your state's limit can't be saved. A flat amount above the minimum can be saved, but on a small charge the fee is cut to the limit for that charge.
- Turning late fees off stops new late fees from that night on. It doesn't remove late fees already charged.
- Opening balances carried over from a previous manager never get a late fee.
- This is not legal advice. If you aren't sure what your declaration allows, ask your association's attorney.
