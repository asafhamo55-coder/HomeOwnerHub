Owners can ask to see the association's records from the portal. **Records requests** lists every request with the deadline your state sets for answering it. The board sees which requests are due soon or late.

## Before you start
- Only board admins and board members can see records requests.
- The deadline uses your association's state. Florida, California and Georgia deadlines are built in. For every other state, the request shows **Check your bylaws**.

## Steps
1. Open **Tickets**, then select [[Records requests]].
2. Each request shows what the owner asked for, the format they prefer, the day it was received and a deadline chip. Red means the deadline has passed. Amber means it is due within 3 days. **Check your bylaws** means no deadline is loaded for your state or for this kind of record.
3. Select **What the statute says** to read the exact words of the law the deadline comes from.
4. Select [[Open conversation]] to reply to the owner. Say when and how they can see the records.
5. If the owner asked for the owner list, select [[Records-request roster]]. It makes the copy of the roster you are allowed to hand over. If they asked for financial records, select [[3-year financial packet]].
6. In the packet, check each year's balance sheet, profit and loss and adopted budget, attach the bank statements it lists as missing, then print it or save it as a PDF.
7. When the owner has the records, select [[Mark fulfilled]]. Optional: note how you provided them. Then select [[Confirm]].

## What happens next
- The request closes and moves to the end of the list as **Fulfilled**. Your note is saved as a private board note on the request.
- The owner is **not** emailed when you mark a request fulfilled. Tell them in the conversation first.
- Every morning, the daily email tells board members how many records requests are past their deadline or due within 3 days, with a link to this page.

## Good to know
- **Florida** (§ 720.303(5)): records must be available within 10 business days of receiving the written request. Minimum damages of $50 a day start on the 11th business day.
- **California** (§ 5210(b)): current-year records are due within 10 business days, and the previous two years within 30 calendar days. Minutes and the membership list follow other sections that aren't loaded, so those show **Check your bylaws**.
- **Georgia** (§ 14-3-1602, for an association incorporated as a Georgia nonprofit corporation): an owner may inspect and copy on a date at least five business days after giving written notice. The date shown is the earliest one. Governing documents, members' meeting minutes from the past three years and what was sent to members are available on notice alone. Board minutes, accounting records and the membership list also need the owner to state a proper purpose "with reasonable particularity". Open **Georgia: which records an owner can ask for** at the top of the page for the exact list. Contracts and other records aren't named, so those show **Check your bylaws**.
- **Georgia from January 1, 2027** (§ 43-17A-7): every Georgia association, incorporated or not, must let owners inspect and copy records on written demand, and give a copy of the certificate of insurance. Requests received from that day cite § 43-17A-7 alongside § 14-3-1602. The new law sets no number of days, so the date shown is still the Nonprofit Code's, and a **Certificate of insurance** request shows **Check your bylaws**. The Secretary of State's proposed rules may add a time limit; they aren't final, so the request shows them as something to confirm. Ask your attorney whether the new law removes the "proper purpose" requirement for accounting records; the app doesn't decide this.
- The **3-year financial packet** covers the last three fiscal years that have ended. It shows totals by account only, with any bank number in an account or file name masked, and only budgets the board adopted. It doesn't make bank statements: it lists the files in **Documents** whose names look like bank statements, and how many months they cover. Nothing is sent from the packet.
- Business days here skip weekends only, not public holidays. The real deadline may be a day or two later than shown, never earlier.
- The deadline counts from the day the owner sent the request in the portal. A request that arrives by mail is not in this list.
- This is general information, not legal advice.
