Most board elections fail on dates and quorum, not on votes. The election planner works out the dates your state sets from the meeting day, keeps the candidate list, and prints a paper ballot kit. There is no online voting.

## Before you start
- Board admins and board members can use the planner. Residents can't see it.
- The election is held at the **Annual** meeting, or at a **Special** meeting where owners vote. Set the meeting's type when you schedule or edit it.
- Have your bylaws at hand. In Georgia the Property Owners' Association Act covers the meeting notice and quorum, but not nominations, ballots or results. Your bylaws decide those.

## Steps
1. Open the meeting from **Meetings** and select [[Plan the election]].
2. Under **Seats and dates**, enter how many seats are up, the day nominations close, and (if owners vote by mailed ballot) the day ballots go out. Select [[Save seats and dates]].
3. Read the **Timeline**. Each step shows the date it's due by and the statute it comes from. A red **Check the dates** means the dates you entered break a rule; the step says how.
4. As nominations come in, add each candidate with their home under **Candidates** and select [[Add candidate]]. Each candidate shows what their home owed on the ledger on the day nominations close.
5. Enter the people who will count the votes under **Tellers** (in California, **Inspectors of elections**).
6. Select [[Open ballot kit]] to print the ballot, the envelope labels and the tally sheet. Copy them for every home.
7. After the meeting, enter the votes under **Results**, with the day you announced them, and select [[Record results]].

## What happens next
The timeline updates as you fill it in. Once you record when the results were announced, it shows when the challenge period ends where your state sets one. Record the result in the minutes as usual.

## If something goes wrong
- The meeting has no **Board election** card: its type is Regular or Emergency. Edit the meeting and set the type to Annual or Special.
- "The saved election couldn't be verified": it wasn't written by a board save, so the planner starts empty. Your next change replaces it.
- If you see **Not saved**, nothing changed. Try again.
- A candidate's home isn't in the list: add it on the Properties page first.

## Good to know
- Georgia: give owners at least 21 days' notice of the annual meeting (7 for a special meeting). Quorum is more than one-third of the votes present at the start, unless your declaration or bylaws say otherwise (O.C.G.A. §§ 44-3-230(a), 44-3-228). For everything else, check your bylaws.
- Florida: a candidate who owes the association anything on the last day to nominate can't be on the ballot. With no more candidates than seats there is no election, and the candidates take their seats even without quorum. A challenge must start within 60 days of the results (Fla. Stat. § 720.306(9)).
- California: one or three independent inspectors, a general notice 30 days before ballots go out, ballots with two envelopes 30 days before voting closes, results within 15 days (Civ. Code §§ 5110, 5115, 5120).
- Texas: votes in a contested board election are written and signed (Prop. Code § 209.0058). Texas's notice and nomination rules aren't loaded, so check your governing documents.
- Balances are shown on screen only. They're never printed or saved with the election.
- The ballot never names the voter. The voter signs only the outer envelope.
- Nothing in the planner sends a message to owners. It's general information, not legal advice.
