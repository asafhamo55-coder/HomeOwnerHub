Georgia counts two waiting periods from the day the owner receives a notice, not from the day you print it. Record how it was mailed and the day it arrived, and HomeownerHub counts from that day.

## Before you start
- Print the letter first. The mailing record belongs to a printed letter.
- Keep the receipt or tracking page. You will need the delivery day.
- Only certified mail and statutory overnight delivery start Georgia's clocks. Other methods can be recorded, but they do not start a clock.

## Steps
1. Open the home, then **Draft collections letter**.
2. Under **Letters printed for this case**, find the notice and select **Record how and when it was mailed**.
3. Choose how it was sent, add the tracking number, and enter the day it was mailed.
4. When the green card or tracking page shows delivery, come back, select **Add or update the mailing record**, and enter **Delivered on**. Select [[Save mailing record]].
5. You can also record a mailing from the case itself: select **Record an action**, pick the notice, and fill in **How and when it was mailed**.

## What happens next
- For the notice before attorney's fees, the owner's 30 days to pay count from the delivery day. Thirty days after it, the attorney's-fee check passes without a warning.
- For the notice before foreclosure, the page shows the earliest foreclosure date: 30 days after delivery, or 60 days for a notice sent from January 1, 2027.
- Entries cannot be edited. A delivery you learn about later is a new entry that links to the first.

## If something goes wrong
- You get "Print the letter first": the mailing record is added to a printed letter.
- You get "It cannot be delivered before it was mailed": check the two dates.
- The page says the method does not start Georgia's clock: only certified mail or statutory overnight delivery does.

## Good to know
- Nothing is sent from HomeownerHub. This records what you did outside it.
- This is not legal advice. Ask your attorney before relying on a date.
