Dues rates are a schedule of what each home pays and the day each amount starts. When you bill dues, the amount starts from the rate in effect, so nobody has to remember last year's figure.

## Before you start
- Board admins and board members manage rates. Owners don't.
- Add the rate your association charges today first, with the day it began. Then add next year's rate with the day it starts.

## Steps
1. Open **Dues** and select [[Dues rates]].
2. Under **Add a rate**, enter the amount per home, how often it's billed and the day it starts.
3. Read the change shown under the form. It compares the new yearly amount with the rate in effect the day before.
4. Select [[Add rate]].
5. When the change is upcoming, check **Upcoming change**. It shows what your state's statute says, if HomeownerHub has it loaded.
6. Select [[Draft owner notice]]. **New message** opens with the notice addressed to owners. Fill in the reason in brackets, check it and send it the usual way.

## What happens next
- From an adopted budget: the last step on the budget's **Adopt this budget** checklist is [[Set the new dues rate]]. It opens Dues rates with the amount filled in: the budget's assessment income divided by your homes, billed monthly, starting the budget year's first day. Change anything before you select [[Add rate]].
- **Generate dues** and **Add a due** start from the rate in effect on the billing day. You can still type a different amount.
- Billing at a different amount than the rate is recorded in the activity log, with the rate and both amounts.
- Bills already made keep their amount.

## If something goes wrong
- "A rate already starts on that day": there's one rate per start day. Pick another day, or remove the other rate if it hasn't started.
- "This rate has already started, so it stays on the schedule": bills were made at it. Add a new rate from a later day instead.
- "The dues change notice didn't come through": go back to Dues rates and select [[Draft owner notice]] again in the same tab.

## Good to know
- **Georgia:** the Property Owners' Association Act (O.C.G.A. §§ 44-3-220 to 44-3-235) sets no notice period, no cap and no owner vote for a dues change. Your declaration and bylaws decide those. At the annual meeting, the board reports the finances and budget projections to owners (§ 44-3-230(a)). While the developer controls the association, the budget and notice of the annual assessment go to owners within 30 days after the fiscal year begins (§ 44-3-232.1(a)(5)).
- **Florida:** an assessment can't be levied at a board meeting unless the meeting notice says assessments will be considered and what they are (§ 720.303(2)(c)2). Each owner's share follows the governing documents (§ 720.308(1)(a)).
- **California:** a regular assessment more than 20% above last fiscal year's needs the approval of a majority of a quorum of members (Civ. Code § 5605(b)). The page warns you when a change is over 20%. The notice period in § 5615 isn't loaded, so check your governing documents.
- **Texas:** if owners vote on an increase, each vote must be in writing and signed (Prop. Code § 209.0058(a)(4)). The meeting and notice rule isn't loaded, so check your governing documents.
- **Other states:** check your governing documents.
- Nothing on Dues rates is sent to anyone. The owner notice goes out only when you send it from Messages.
- Owners can see the rate in effect today for their own association, not upcoming rates.
- The page quotes statutes as text. It isn't legal advice. Check your governing documents and ask your association's attorney.
