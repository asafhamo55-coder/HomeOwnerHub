Use this after a fiscal year ends, when your CPA asks for the year's records to prepare the association's tax return or review.

## Before you start
- Board admins and board members can download the package. Residents can't.
- Reconcile each bank account through the last day of the year first. The package shows any account that isn't.
- Record each vendor's W-9 on its **Compliance** page, so the 1099 list shows it as on file.

## Steps
1. Open **Accounting**, select the [[Reports]] tab, then select [[Year-end package]].
2. Under **Period**, pick the fiscal year, then select [[Apply]]. The page opens on the most recent year that has ended.
3. Check the preview: the **Form 1120-H worksheet**, the **1099-NEC candidates** and the bank reconciliation status.
4. Select [[Download year-end package]]. A zip file is saved to your computer.
5. Send the zip to your CPA.

## What happens next
The zip has one spreadsheet each for the trial balance, income statement, balance sheet, general ledger detail, amounts owed by each home at year end, bank reconciliation status, vendor payments, budget vs. actual, the Form 1120-H worksheet and the 1099-NEC candidates, plus a README. Each download is recorded in the activity log, with the year and row counts.

## If something goes wrong
- "Only board members and admins can download the year-end package.": ask a board admin to download it.
- "Download failed": nothing was saved. Try again. If it keeps failing, refresh the page first.
- A bank account says **Not reconciled through year end**: reconcile it on **Bank**, then download again.

## Good to know
- The package isn't tax advice. It doesn't decide whether the association qualifies for or should file Form 1120-H, or what it owes.
- On the 1120-H worksheet, assessments and dues count as exempt function income, and interest as income that isn't. Late fees, fines, rentals and other income are left blank for the CPA to classify. Lines that depend on them stay blank until the CPA does.
- The 1099-NEC list covers January 1 to December 31 of the year the fiscal year ends, and shows vendors paid $600 or more. For payments made after 2025 the federal threshold is $2,000, so the list also flags vendors paid over $2,000. HomeownerHub doesn't store whether a vendor is a corporation, so verify each W-9. Tax ID numbers are never included.
