When a home is being sold or refinanced, the title company or lender asks the association for an **estoppel certificate**: what the owner owes, what comes due next, and any open violations. HomeownerHub fills it in from the home's records. You fill in what the app doesn't know, check the figures, and print it or save it as a PDF.

## Before you start
- Board admins and board members can prepare a certificate. Residents can't open the page.
- Record every payment first. The association may be held to the amounts on the certificate.
- The home needs a linked unit. Dues are recorded on the unit, so without one the certificate shows no charges.

## Steps
1. Open **Properties** and open the home.
2. Select the **⋯** menu next to [[Edit]], then [[Prepare estoppel certificate]].
3. Under **The request**, enter the day the request was received. In Florida, choose how it will be delivered and tick [[Requested on an expedited basis]] if it was.
4. Under **Fill in the blanks**, fill in each item the app doesn't hold: the requestor, parcel designation, transfer or capital contribution fee, the fee you charge and who signs. Florida adds the parking space, transfer approval, right of first refusal, other associations and insurance contacts. Texas and Georgia add their own items (see below).
5. Read the certificate to the bottom. Anything still missing shows as a highlighted **BLANK**.
6. Tick [[I’ve verified these figures]].
7. Select [[Print / Save as PDF]]. Choose your printer, or **Save as PDF** to send the file yourself.

## What happens next
- The certificate shows the balance owed on the date of issuance, each unpaid charge, charges not yet due, the regular assessment and how often it's billed, the date it's paid through, special assessments, open violations, any active payment plan and the collections stage.
- In Florida (§ 720.30851), the page counts the deadline: 10 business days after the request, skipping weekends and federal holidays. The certificate is effective for 30 days if delivered by hand or e-mail, or 35 days by regular mail. Charges that come due in that period are marked.
- In Florida, the fee is capped at $250, plus up to $150 if the account is delinquent and $100 if it was expedited and delivered within 3 business days. The state adjusts these caps for inflation every 5 years, so check the Department of Business and Professional Regulation's current amounts. After the 10-business-day deadline, no fee may be charged. A payment not yet applied to a charge counts against the past-due amount, so an account whose payments cover what's past due isn't delinquent.
- In California, the page makes the § 4525(a)(4) statement of assessments and unpaid amounts and a summary of open violation notices. It's labelled **Partial**: the resale packet also needs governing documents, the annual budget disclosures and other documents, which the page lists. The deadline is 10 days (§ 4530).
- In Texas (Property Code § 207.003), the page makes a **resale certificate**. The deadline is 10 business days after the written request, and any proof of the requestor's authority you asked for, is received and verified. The certificate shows the regular assessment, the total owed and the open violations on record. You fill in the rest of what § 207.003(b) asks for: any right of first refusal, approved special assessments not yet billed, capital expenditures, reserves, judgments, lawsuits, other known violations, government code notices, the administrative transfer fee, all transfer fees, the managing agent and whether the restrictions allow foreclosure. It's labelled **Partial**: you also attach the restrictions, the bylaws and rules, the operating budget and balance sheet, and the insurance certificate. The fee is capped at $375, and none may be charged if the certificate is late. Texas condominiums are not covered by this section.
- In Georgia, there is no estoppel statute like Florida's. The page makes a **statement of amounts due** under the Georgia Property Owners' Association Act, § 44-3-232(d): the assessments past due and unpaid, with late charges and interest. The deadline is 5 business days from receiving the written request. If it's missed, the association's lien is extinguished as to the buyer's or lender's interest. You confirm the association is submitted to the Act; if it isn't, the page won't print the statement. The fee is at most $10.00, and only if your declaration provides for one; otherwise enter $0. Charges that fall due within the 5 business days are marked.
- In other states, you get a plain account status certificate with no statutory claims.
- Each certificate you open, and each one you print, is logged with ids and amounts only.
- Nothing is emailed. HomeownerHub doesn't send the certificate to anyone.

## If something goes wrong
- [[Print / Save as PDF]] is greyed out: a BLANK is still empty, the fee is above the cap, or [[I’ve verified these figures]] isn't ticked. In Georgia, it's also greyed out if you answered No to the Property Owners' Association Act, or entered a fee your declaration doesn't provide for. The message beside the button says which.
- "Couldn’t record this certificate": the log couldn't be written, so nothing was printed. Try again.
- "Figures changed since this page loaded — reload": a payment or charge was recorded, or the day changed, after you opened the page. Reload the page, check the figures and print again. Your BLANK entries need to be filled in again.
- The printout says "DRAFT — NOT VALID — print from the Print button": you printed from the browser (for example with Cmd+P or File > Print). Only [[Print / Save as PDF]] on the page prints the certificate.
- "Paid through" is a BLANK: the app can't tell how often dues are billed from one charge. Enter the date yourself.

## Good to know
- Payment plan installments are listed as plan installments, not special assessments.
- "Business day" isn't defined in the Florida, Texas or Georgia sections. The count skips weekends and federal holidays. Allow for any other day your office is closed, such as a state holiday.
- This page doesn't prepare a Texas update to a resale certificate (§ 207.003(f): 7 business days, $75 cap).
- The page quotes statutes as text. It isn't legal advice: check your governing documents and ask your association's attorney.
