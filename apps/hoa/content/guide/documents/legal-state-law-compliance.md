Use **State Law** when you need to know what your state's HOA law says, not just your own CC&Rs. Answers cite the statute section they came from.

## Before you start
- Board members and admins use this page from the **Knowledge** section of the sidebar.
- It works for associations in Georgia, Florida, California and Texas. Your association's state comes from its setup.
- The statutes for your state must be loaded first. If they aren't, you see "Statutes aren’t loaded yet". Contact support to load them.

## Steps
1. Open **State Law** from the sidebar. The **Overview** tab shows your state's code, how many sections are indexed, and recent law updates.
2. To ask a question, select [[Ask a question]]. Type your question, or pick one under **Try asking**, then select [[Ask]].
3. Read the answer, its confidence level and the **Cited statutes**. Select a citation to read that section's full text.
4. To read the law yourself, open the [[Statutes]] tab. Pick a topic such as **Meetings & elections** or **Fines & enforcement**, or [[All topics]], then open a section.
5. On a section's page, [[Ask about this law]] takes you back to the question box. **Official source** links to the state's own copy.
6. To share a change in the law, open [[Law updates]] and select [[Post update]]. Fill in **Headline** and **Summary** (at least 20 characters), then select [[Post update]].
7. Florida associations: open the [[Compliance]] tab to see which official records are on file, which owners can see, and whether recent and upcoming meetings were added early enough for notice.

## What happens next
- A live law update shows under **Recent law updates** on the State law page for every board in your state, not just yours. Use [[Archive]] to hide it, and [[Restore]] to bring it back.
- The Florida check updates every time you open it. Fix a missing record by selecting **Upload it**, or open the meeting to check its notice.

## If something goes wrong
- "Your state isn’t covered yet": your association isn't in Georgia, Florida, California or Texas, or its state isn't set. If you see [[Finish setup]], finish setting up your association first.
- "Couldn’t get an answer": nothing was saved, and your question is still in the box. Select [[Try again]].
- "Question is too short." or "Question is too long (>2000 chars).": ask in at least 3 and at most 2,000 characters.
- "No statutes loaded yet" on the Statutes tab: your state's text hasn't been loaded for your association. Contact support.
- "Available for Florida associations" on the Compliance tab: the check follows Florida's § 720.303 only, so it doesn't apply to other states.

## Good to know
- Every page says "Informational, not legal advice." Answers are grounded in the statute text, but they don't replace an attorney. For decisions about your association, consult a licensed attorney in your state.
- If the statutes don't cover your question, the assistant says so instead of guessing.
- The Florida check is "an aid for your board, not a certification of compliance." It only sees what is on file in HomeownerHub, not documents or notices posted anywhere else.
- HomeownerHub doesn't record when a meeting notice was posted. The check only knows when the meeting was added, so you need to confirm the notice yourself.
- Deleting a law update removes it for every board in your state and can't be undone. Archive it instead if you may need it again.
- For questions about your own CC&Rs and bylaws, use [Ask the Docs](guide:documents-governing-and-ask) instead.
