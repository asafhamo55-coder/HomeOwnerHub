**Compliance** lists every state-law obligation HomeownerHub tracks for your association, says where each one stands, and keeps the document that proves the board met it.

## Before you start
- Only board members and admins see the page.
- The list depends on your association's state in its setup. Georgia has the longest list.
- Upload the proof first, for example the Secretary of State receipt, under **Documents**.

## Steps
1. Open [[Compliance]] in the sidebar.
2. Read the red banner at the top, if there is one. It lists what's overdue. Select an item to jump to it.
3. Under each obligation, read its status, the steps, and **What the statute says**. That's the law's own words.
4. For a deadline you've met, pick the proof under **Evidence**, then select [[Mark as done]].
5. To add or change the proof later, pick another document and select [[Save evidence]].

## What happens next
- The obligation shows **Done** with the date. The evidence links to the document.
- Marking it done here also clears it from the dashboard's State law deadlines card and the morning email.
- The monthly board report has a **State law compliance** section: what's overdue, what's coming up and the most pressing item. The board packet lists every obligation with its status.

## If something goes wrong
- "That document isn’t in your association’s documents.": pick a document from your own Documents.
- "Mark it as done first, then attach the evidence.": the deadline isn't done yet. Select [[Mark as done]] instead.
- "Couldn’t load your meetings": a loading problem, not "no meeting". Refresh the page.
- "Couldn’t load your elections": the same kind of loading problem. Refresh the page.
- **Director elections** says "No election planned in HomeownerHub": open the annual meeting and plan the election in its election planner. Only elections saved there appear.

## Good to know
- **Georgia:** registering with the Secretary of State, then renewing every year and amending after an officer change. Also the annual members' meeting with 21 days' notice, minutes and financial records, and a statement of amounts due within five business days. The last three come from the Property Owners' Association Act, which covers property submitted to it. If yours isn't, check your governing documents.
- **Georgia annual meeting:** hold it before your fiscal year ends. If the board doesn't, owners holding 5% of the voting power (or your bylaws' figure, never more than 25%) may call it themselves.
- **Director elections (Georgia, Florida, California, Texas):** one row for each election in the election planner. It shows the next date on the election's timeline, dates that break the rules, and whether the results are recorded. A date that has passed says to check it was done, because HomeownerHub can't tell.
- **California:** the row turns red if the results notice is more than 15 days late and not recorded as sent.
- **Georgia, after the results are recorded:** a row to amend your Secretary of State registration within 30 days if the officers changed. HomeownerHub counts from the day the results were recorded; if the board picks officers later, count from that day. Before Jan 1, 2027 it says to list the new officers in your first registration. Check the Secretary of State's guidance.
- **Texas:** the management certificate. **Florida:** director education certificates (tracked on Members). **California:** the annual budget report window (from the budget's adoption checklist).
- The monthly board report adds a **Director elections** line with the most pressing election item.
- **Georgia condominiums:** if your association is set up as a condo, the annual meeting, records and statement rows come from the Georgia Condominium Act instead: 21 days' notice stating the time, place and purpose (§ 44-3-102), minutes and financial records (§ 44-3-106(d)), and the statement of amounts due within five business days (§ 44-3-109(d)). The 5% rule above doesn't apply. Instead, a condominium instrument recorded on or after July 1, 1990 must let 15% of the unit owners call a meeting.
- **Georgia condominiums, insurance:** a row lists the cover the Act requires: property insurance for the full replacement cost of the buildings, general liability of at least $1 million per occurrence and $2 million aggregate, and the certificate of insurance within five business days when a unit owner asks about a water claim (§ 44-3-107).
- The registration rows are the same for an HOA and a condo: SB 406 covers condominium associations too.
- A Georgia association set up as an HOA still gets a "Georgia Condominium Act rules" row that says to check your governing documents.
- The page quotes statutes word for word. It isn't legal advice. Ask your association's attorney.
